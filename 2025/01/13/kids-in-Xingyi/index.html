kids-in-Xingyi

  2025-01-13     Travel

It was freezing cold outside, and I was walking outside of the scenery park, completely on my own. My teammates, who promised to have dinner with me, thought I would not come to them, so they marched away without noticing me when I was in the bathroom. The restaurant was around three kilometers away, and I had no choice but to take the bus to the restaurant. Finding the nearest bus stop was the elementary school of Leli, I headed straight to the main avenue and reached the bus stop, with the sense of lonely growing as the sky was darkening.

As soon as I reached the bus stop, the students of the elementary school had already fled from the campus. Lots of them packed themselves into the bus stop, but a few of the girls were playing jumping the ropes. Eight years old or so, the girls were thin, with dark faces. A tint of red penetrated their cheeks and made itself visible to us. They wore their down coats, which had a vivid color but were slightly worn and torn, but they did not release the red tie from their neck. It seemed like they had just returned from school and joyfully took off their school uniform. The vibrant energy emitted from their sweat and laughter impressed me, so I stopped and began watching them in awe.

The students were playing an old game, which was popular when my mother was at their age. Three girls used their legs to tauten a loop of rope, and other students swiftly jumped across the rope. They cheerfully jumped over and over again, and the loop was lowered and lifted regularly. As the game went by, they began to perform more challenging moves. Jumping to the side, they curled their legs just in time so that both legs drew a rainbow-like curve and crossed the rope narrowly. They also tried to assign some meaning to their movement, as I have seen them yelling “I would give myself some anesthetics” when she claimed to have a second chance for her leg touching the string. This kind of activity also reminds me of my childhood, when I would come across different holes in our playground and pretend I had teleported myself somewhere else. I would really want to get into their conversation and ask their permission to shoot, so I hesitantly stopped them and asked in an uncertain tone:

“Would you please let me finish my assignment and shoot a small video for you?”

Seeing the confusion on their face, I started to explain my identity as a Jiao Tong University Student. I rushed to find proof to show I was a student, but I could only see the marks on MySJTU. The girls went into silence. They knew they should not trust strangers, for they had the chance to be seduced. After what seemed to be an eternity, I heard a young voice,

“Yeah, Okay!”

Together with the most brilliant smile emerged on the face of the youngsters. Her partner tried to stop her by sealing her mouth with her hand, but she fled away, her laughter lingering around the air with the smell of simple food. They started to jump the ropes again to let me shoot them, with even more vigor and happiness.
![The rope-jumping students][images/Kids-in-Xingyi.jpg]
As the sound of the engine came closer and closer, the students began twittering louder and louder, and they began to gather in front of the stop, jumping in coldness. The girls happily folded the rope and asked me to get on the bus with them in an exciting tone, as if we were heading for a new adventure. With a loud noise of releasing gas, the door banged open, and the students torrented into the bus like a school of fish. The cabin was dark as the light was not turned on. The odor of diesel attacked my nose, suggesting the bus was an old one. The students hopped onto the seats, with their hands holding packages of small snakes—the ones you could buy with just 50 cents. They started to enjoy the snack on the bus while I was standing at the door, trying to pay for the ticket.

I asked one of the young boys, “What is this? Is this spicy stick?”

“No, it is small chicken wing. Try one!” He took out one from the cheap plastic package. It was a small piece of fried pastry in an ovoid shape, which was the only similarity of the food to an actual chicken wing. I put it in my mouth and tasted it. Ordinary BBQ flavor with a bit of adjustment. Not something special.

I carefully looked through the young lad, while my hands were busy operating the mobile to pay for the ticket. He wore a think school uniform jacket that was definitely in need to be washed thoroughly. Being as curious as I was, he asked me in a clear voice,

“Hi brother, are you an academic student?”

Academic student? Given I had been admitted into a college, I guess so. “Yes, I am a student from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, a university student.”

He looked excited and yelled to his friend,” A university student from Shanghai!”
![A student holding firework in Xingyi][images/Firework.jpg]

His friend looked at me, giggling,” Why does a university student come to ask us elementary student questions?” They started twittering again. The bus went through the stations, and I began to worry about whether I could make it to pay for the ticket before I went off the bus.

He started to said again, with his eyes staring on my mobile. “I know there is a way to pay by only scanning one QR code.”

But I was about to finish. I nodded to him and thanked him again, and I noticed there was no sign of regret in his eyes, instead, he was genuinely happy to see my problem being solved. He told his friend to move the backpack of his friend’s and asked me to be seated, but I declined politely for I would get off at the next stop, Sujiatang. As I was heading towards the end of the bus, I noticed that it was almost dark outside. Craving to have more time to talk with them, I nodded to them, weaved goodbye to them on the bus, and turned around to get off the bus.

“Brother.” He suddenly stopped me as I was heading towards the exit, hands holding tight the handles.

I turned around and what he said just surprised me.

“We shall meet again!”

I was completely moved by his words, as he showed much more kindness and love for a student he had just known. I froze at the door, eyes looking back, the mixed share of feelings emerging from the deepest of my heart, pooling up my eyes and nose. I tried my best to hold it back before I was thrown into the windy night of Xingyi again. I don’t know if he heard my final reply “Yes, we shall.” because I had no faith about its volume, but I knew that we would meet again, in my memories, for I would use this precious memory, these cute kids, to cure me whenever I was in the abyss of despair and sadness.

This is my story with the elementary students in Xingyi.
